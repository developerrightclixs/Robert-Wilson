A complete guide to racing sponsorship deals

Guide November 13, 2026 Robert Alexander 6 min read

Racing sponsorship can do far more than put a logo in front of a crowd. Done well, it gives your business a season-long marketing platform, with branding, promotions, hospitality, and content all working toward the same goals. This guide walks through how deals are structured and what to look for before you sign.

What a sponsorship package includes

Every package is different, but most combine some of the following:

  • Branding: logo placement on the race car, hauler, fire suit, and team apparel.
  • Appearances: driver visits to your stores, trade shows, or company events.
  • Hospitality: race-day passes, garage tours, and suites for customers and staff.
  • Digital content: social posts, video, and behind-the-scenes stories featuring your brand.
  • Usage rights: permission to use team and driver imagery in your own marketing.

Primary vs. associate sponsorship

A primary sponsor gets the most prominent placement, usually the hood and quarter panels, for one or more races. Associate sponsors take smaller placements across more races at a lower cost. Many brands start as an associate, learn what works, and move up.

Your logo on the race car is just the beginning. The value comes from what you build around it.

How pricing works

Cost depends on the series, the team’s profile, the size and position of your placement, and how many races you commit to. A single regional short-track race can fit a small marketing budget, while a national primary sponsorship is a significant investment. Activation (the promotions and events that use the sponsorship) should be budgeted alongside the deal itself.

Setting goals you can measure

Before any contract is signed, agree on what success looks like. Useful measures include:

  1. Audience reach at the track, on broadcast, and on social channels.
  2. Customer inquiries and leads generated by race-day promotions.
  3. Promotion and coupon redemptions tied to the sponsorship.
  4. Relationships strengthened through hospitality events.

Questions to ask before you sign

Ask exactly which deliverables are included, how and when results will be reported, what happens if a race is rained out, and what rights you have to use team and driver imagery. A good partner will answer all of these clearly and put them in writing.
